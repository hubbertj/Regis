2.3.2 Themes in the Practice of Hinduism

Perhaps comparing Hinduism to one's own faith highlights what a unique situation exists with Sanatana Dharma. It is often confusing to try and understand how something so unorganized can satisfy the spiritual impulses of a vast collection of individuals, and also why they consider themselves integrally related to one another. This is not due solely to inhabiting the same subcontinent.

There are a number of consistent themes which run throughout the various forms of practice:

  1. the goal is to improve life and overcome suffering.
  2. all the practices to reduce suffering consider self discipline crucial.
  3. observing and working with the self is the basic practice.
  4. the path is a search for truth, dharma.
  5. liberation is the goal.
  6. it is possible to achieve liberation and all the different paths can lead to it.
  7. there is a natural order which is reflected in society and individual lives.
  8. life's circumstances are the result of karma, and therefore, the individual's responsibility.
  9. since every thought, deed and action is interconnected, each person has the responsibility of the family, society and cosmos, thus contributing to the well-being of others.
  10. surrendering attachment brings an end to suffering.
  11. religion and social structure are integrated (Fisher, 2002, p. 125, 127).
