2.3 The Nature of Brahman, Ultimate Reality, and the Self, Atman.

Although, as implied by the quote from Gandhi, there are diverse beliefs in Hinduism, the ultimate source, the cosmic power, the sacred energy at the core of everything, is called Brahman. It is that which unifies and is the basis of all phenomena. In the selections in Van Voorst this week, you read a selection titled, "You are that." The definition of Brahman should help clarify its meaning.

Following are selected statements by Huston Smith, a great teacher of comparative religion, who came to know and appreciate different world views by dedicating himself not only to the study of texts and artifacts but also by actively exploring the cultures in which they manifested and personally engaging in their spiritual practices. These statements may help you form a better idea of Brahman.

  1. "The invisible excludes nothing, the invisible that excludes nothing is the infinite – the soul of India is the infinite."
  2. "Philosophers tell us that the Indians were the first ones to conceive of a true infinite from which nothing is excluded. The West shied away from this notion. The West likes form, boundaries that distinguish and demarcate. The trouble is that boundaries also imprison – they restrict and confine."
  3. "India saw this clearly and turned her face to that which has no boundary whatever.""India anchored her soul in the infinite, seeing the things of the world as masks of the infinite. One assumes – there can be no end to these masks, of course. If they express a true infinity." And it is here that India's mind boggling variety links up to her infinite soul."
  4. "India includes so much because her soul being infinite excludes nothing." It goes without saying that the universe that India saw emerging from the infinite was stupendous."
  5. While the West was still thinking, perhaps, of a 6,000 years old universe – India was already envisioning ages and eons and galaxies as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. The Universe so vast that modern astronomy slips into its folds without a ripple." (The Mystic's Journey - India and the Infinite: The Soul of a People – By Huston Smith http://www.hinduwisdom.info/quotes41_60.htm)

The Self, that which is our continual reference point, that which defines our individual existence, has multiple layers in Hinduism. Each person has a soul which is the source of that individual's uniqueness. This "jiva" includes our particular body and personality. The main quality of this "jiva" or gross soul is that it is constantly changing: we age, get ill and healthy, gain and lose weight, have accidents that affect our body parts, etc. However, at the deepest level of one's self, is the Atman, inner soul, which, when one understands its nature completely, is identical with Brahman.
