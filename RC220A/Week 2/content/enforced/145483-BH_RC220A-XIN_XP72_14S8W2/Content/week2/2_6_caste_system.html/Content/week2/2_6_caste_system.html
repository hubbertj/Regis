2.6 The Caste System

The first era of Hindu worship and belief is associated with the early Vedas and was characterized by ritual and sacrifice. The Brahmans, who were originally the keepers and performers of the sacred rituals, became more and more influential in shaping society and defining its ethical system. They became the leaders in the era of Hinduism known as the Way of Action which gave birth to the Laws of Manu, written between 200 BCE and CE 100. These introduced and articulated the caste system

"Hinduism, therefore, is perhaps the most catholic of all religions, for it has not become so in the course of its evolution but was based on the principle of catholicity from the beginnings. Those who laid down the code of Manu, made provision both for different mentalities and different vocations in the most thorough going manner; they showed an understanding of the social organism which in subsequent times has seldom been equaled"

(The Legacy of Asia and Western Man - By Allan Watts p.28-29).

"Material progress is not to be mistaken for inner progress. When technology outstrips moral development, the prospect is not that of a millennium but of extinction. Our ancient heritage is a potent antidote to the current tendency to standardize souls and seek salvation in herds"

(India's Priceless Heritage - By Nani A. Palkhivala 1980 p.1).

Castes are rigid, exclusive social groups with hereditary membership in the caste into which one is born. Caste implies that one only marries members of the same caste; does not have personal contact with members of other castes; selects a predetermined occupation; and accepts a fixed place in society. There are three main ideas underlying this system.

  • To be born into the higher castes implies that one lived righteously according to caste dharma in previous lives
  • Social hierarchy is a reflection of the hierarchy expressed in the natural order, Rita.
  • Ritual purity or impurity is an important distinction. For instance, a Brahman will not share food or drink with a member of a lower class. Members of the upper castes consider the lowest castes to be ritually unclean. Marrying someone from a different caste, although not officially outlawed, is generally not recognized.

Originally created by the Brahman priests, the caste system became part of Hindu religious law, verified by the claim of divine revelation.

We call America the land of opportunity and we pride ourselves on everyone being able to achieve the American dream. We are fiercely protective of our upward mobility. Take a candid look at American society today. Statistics show that the upper and lower classes are increasing and that the American middle class is decreasing. They also show that the gap between the upper class and the middle class is widening significantly. Do we have anything resembling a caste system? Have black and white citizens always had equal opportunities? How about women, have they always been equal? (n.b.Women only won the right to vote in 1920,and the Equal Rights Amendment was voted down in the 1970's.)

In India social stratification still exists. Beginning in the twentieth century strides were made towards eradicating the unjust social and economic aspects of the caste system. In 1949, "untouchability" was legally abolished under Article 17 of the Indian Constitution. Educational and employment opportunities for the untouchables have opened, but intermarriage is not socially acceptable. In the urban areas, although the caste system has weakened, it is rare to find castes mingling socially. So, there is some relaxation of caste boundaries in urban areas, but they remain firmly entrenched in the village culture of rural India.
