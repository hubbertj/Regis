2.4 The Key Beliefs of Sanatana Dharma

Maya, samsara, karma, reincarnation, and moksha are the key spiritual principles in Hinduism. They were defined and set forth in the Upanishads, the later Vedic texts, which turned the spiritual attention of Hindus to philosophic contemplation of the nature of ultimate reality, the natural order, and the nature of self and the nature of death.

We human beings see our selves and our world through the perspective of Maya, which means illusion. We relate to the world as if each phenomenon is individual, solid and unchangeable. From the Hindu view this is illusory because everything is related, partaking of one supreme reality, which constantly changes and manifests in endless different forms.

Starting with this mistaken perspective, we are caught in an endless cycle of rebirth, with its accompanying suffering, Samsara. We long for the cycle to end, to achieve liberation from it, Moksha. It is our original misunderstanding and attachment to these transient phenomena, including our Self which keeps the cycle in motion. It is Karma, action and subsequent reaction, the energy resulting from our actions, which determines our rebirths and the circumstances of each life.
