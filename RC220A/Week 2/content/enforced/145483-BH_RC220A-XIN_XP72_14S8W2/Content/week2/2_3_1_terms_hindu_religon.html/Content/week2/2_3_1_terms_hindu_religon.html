2.3.1 Terms in the Hindu Religion

As has been noted, the Hindu religion has a wide range of beliefs:

  • advaita - everything partakes of the divine or supreme reality
  • monotheism - one supreme, all- knowing deity
  • trimurti - three aspects make up the one supreme god or ultimate reality: Brahma the creator, Vishnu the sustainer, and Shiva destroyer. As Molloy says, "In Western religions, creation is associated with a good God and destruction comes from what is not god – from sin or evil or Satan. In Hinduism, however, destruction is considered to be simply another part of the divine energy at work in the world. Destruction is a type of recycling, the necessary loss of form, which occurs so that new forms may appear; and death is always thought of as leading to a new life" (p 96).
  • polytheism - multiple deities, often conceived of as a pantheon with major and minor figures, each with an area of power and effectiveness.
  • Animism - all objects have a spirit. This form of worship is found predominantly in rural areas where the challenges of life are tied to nature.

Think About This

  • Do you have difficulty conceiving of these factors as the components of a single religion?
  • Can you imagine a family home in which each bedroom may have a shrine dedicated to a different deity, and no one is disturbed about the diversity?
  • Can you appreciate that, rather than leading to confusion, this diversity leads to a rich and lively culture?
