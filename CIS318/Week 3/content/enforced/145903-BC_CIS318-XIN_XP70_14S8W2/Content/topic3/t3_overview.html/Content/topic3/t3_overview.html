Topic 3 . Computer & Internet Crime and Privacy

Topic Overview

This week’s topic presents a discussion concerning computer and Internet crime and privacy.

Identity theft, hacking, computer viruses, theft of computer equipment, and the like are all examples of the formidable array of computer- and Internet-related crimes that businesses and individuals face. The ease of access to software and hardware by perpetrators serves to make crimes against businesses and individuals more prevalent. Safeguarding private and business information is essential; however, ethical issues concerning security must be taken into consideration.

Privacy is another concern, compounded by rapidly advancing electronic technology. A business must clearly define and provide unambiguous expectations regarding personal and private information. A business needs to have strict guidelines on who has access to the private information. Further, businesses must take steps to protect private information from intruders.

This week's content will delve into these areas all within the context of ethics and ethical decision making.

Learning Outcomes/Objectives

At the end of this topic, you will be able to:

  • Examine types of security incidents related to computer crime and identify the perpetrators of computer crime.
  • Identify at least three means to detect, minimize, and stop computer crime in the business environment.
  • Explain key issues related to privacy from both an employee and employer perspective.
  • Define individual privacy.
  • Review 15 United States government and European Union privacy laws, acts, and directives.  
  • Discuss the differences between United States and European Union approaches to privacy, these being self-regulation and strict government regulation, respectively.
  • Identify key privacy issues, such as identity theft or spyware, and discuss recommendations concerning how IT professionals can minimize or thwart such issues from adversely affecting personal and customer data in IT systems.
