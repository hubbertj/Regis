Protecting Yourself Online Video Discussion

Context: Internet privacy may be an oxymoron. Can users ever be 100% sure companies they deal with on the web are protecting their privacy? We have secure shopping and banking environments available from most companies. We see this security reflected by the “https” at the beginning of a URL, privacy notices, and secure sockets layer (SSL) certification provided by companies like VeriSign. However, the question remains, are we completely secure?

The video from CNN discusses privacy related to the social networking website Facebook. The video discusses updates required by Facebook users to their private settings due to the enhancement and addition of new privacy settings. A risk appears related to users selecting the default settings, which may not be the most secure choice. Further, there appear to be third-party developers who provide quizzes, polls, and “snowball fights” who can gain access to users’ personal information when users use these applications.

The overarching concern is that Facebook, and perhaps other such sites, do not fully disclose, or make transparent, the fact that these third-party developers can obtain users’ personal information. In addition, there is a concern that Facebook will not implement the default settings in a way that provides users with a secure and private online environment.

Reviewing this video will help you gain a better understanding of the importance of privacy in the context of ethical behavior.

Case Study Discussion

Context: Computer and Internet crime can have major ramifications for business. Information can be stolen, assets can be taken, and reputations can be damaged. The following case study demonstrates the lack of adequate enforcement of ethical standards can result in catastrophe. Understanding that having ethics standards is a very different matter from enforcing ethics standards is an important lesson for business.

Case Study #1: Crime

Topic 3 Crime Case Study

Peter Gumble, European editor for Fortune magazine, comments:

Kerviel is a stunning example of a trader breaking the rules, but he’s by no means alone. One of the dirty little secrets of trading floors around the world is that every so often, somebody is caught concealing a position and is quickly – and quietly – dismissed…. [This] might be shocking for people unfamiliar with the macho, high-risk, high-reward culture of most trading floors, but consider this: the only way banks can tell who will turn into a good trader and who won’t is by giving every youngster it hires a chance to show his mettle. That means allowing even the most junior traders to take aggressive positions. This leeway is supposed to be matched by careful controls, but clearly they aren’t foolproof.

  1. What is your reaction to this statement by Mr. Gumble?
  2. What explanation can there be for the failure of SocGen’s internal control system to detect Kerviel’s transactions while Eurex detected many suspicious transactions?

Written Assignment

Context: Computer crime is deemed unethical behavior. Such crimes, when successfully perpetrated against sensitive or private information, can shatter a business’s or organization’s reputation. Further, these attacks can adversely affect profits, market share, and the public’s trust.

Task Description: Research a recent (within the last five years) case of computer crime against a U.S. or international company or organization in which private information was stolen via electronic means. Provide a short (at least two paragraphs) description of what happened. In your description, include the name of the company or organization, the industry (such as medical, manufacturing, etc.), how the computer crime was perpetrated, who perpetrated the crime (insider, terrorist, hacker, etc.), state whether the company or organization reported the crime or whether the crime was discovered/reported by someone else, and what private information was stolen.

Next, describe the impact of the loss of this private information on the company or organization. For example, did the company or organization: lose market share, was the company or organization sued, was there a strong public outcry against the company or organization, did the company or organization have to pay large sums of money, did the incident become a huge public relations nightmare for the company or organization, etc. In your opinion, was the company’s or organization’s response/action sufficient? Do you think the response/action minimized any adverse impact on the company or organization?

Finally, identify at least three means the company or organization could have used to detect, minimize, and stop this computer crime. Briefly explain each means and provide your analysis regarding why you believe each means would have been effective.

Delivery: Write a 2-3 page paper. Content, quality of writing, use of APA formatting, use of in-text citations from your resources, and effective writing are all elements of your grade. To complete this assignment, post your paper to the Dropbox by midnight (MT) Saturday of the week the assignment is due.
