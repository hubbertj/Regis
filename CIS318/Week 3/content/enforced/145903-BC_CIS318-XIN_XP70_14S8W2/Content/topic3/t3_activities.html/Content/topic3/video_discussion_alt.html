Protecting Your Privacy on the Internet

[Please note: it is not necessary to view and listen to the video. The relevant points are summarized below.]

Context

Internet privacy, like military intelligence, may be an oxymoron. Can users ever be 100 percent sure companies they deal with on the web are protecting their privacy? We have secure shopping and banking environments available from most companies. We see this security reflected by the “https” at the beginning of a URL, privacy notices, and secure sockets layer (SSL) certification provided by companies like VeriSign. However, the question remains, are we completely secure?

The video from CNN discusses privacy related to the social networking website facebook. The video discusses updates required by facebook users to their private settings due to the enhancement and addition of new privacy settings. A risk appears related to users selecting the default settings, which may not be the most secure choice. Further, there appear to be third-party developers who provide quizzes, polls, and “snowball fights” who can gain access to users’ personal information when users use these applications.

The overarching concern is that facebook, and perhaps other such sites, do not fully disclose, or make transparent, the fact these third-party developers can obtain users’ personal information. In addition, there is a concern that facebook will not implement the default settings in a way that provides users with a secure and private online environment.

Task description

Discuss the following questions in the Discussion Forum.

Forum Discussion

  1. Do you think websites such as facebook have a responsibility to inform users about potential dangers concerning their privacy? Why or why not?
  2. What websites have you visited that provided full transparency of such things as facebook’s use of third-party developers who can obtain your personal information? What about websites that were not very transparent?
  3. What two methods do you think websites should use to help users safeguard their privacy? Briefly discuss your rationale.
