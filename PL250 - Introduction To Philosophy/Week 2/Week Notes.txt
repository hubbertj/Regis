PL250: Introduction to Philosophy
Topic 2: The Examined Life
Topic Overview

mood imageHave you ever encountered a person who challenged your most cherished assumptions, someone who insisted that you support your positions with reasoned arguments instead of platitudes and truisms? Socrates was that person in ancient Greece. He likened his role in society to that of a “gadfly” because he irritated his fellow Athenians by challenging them to wake up from their intellectual slumbers and to think for themselves. In so doing, he charted a new path for philosophical reasoning and its applicability in everyday life. His footprint in the history of philosophy is indelible, for in many respects, his methodology set the stage for the “formal birth of Western philosophy” (Chaffee, 2013, p. 55). But it was a legacy for which he paid a heavy price. As his student Plato explains in one of the most-read pieces of philosophical literature, The Apology, Socrates was put on trial and eventually sentenced to death for the “crime” of challenging accepted wisdom. (The English word “apology” has more than one meaning, but its philosophical sense is derived from the Greek word apologia, which is a defense. Thus, The Apology is the story of Socrates’ defense of his life and work.) In this topic, we will explore his philosophical ideas and methodologies and assess its relevance for contemporary life.

Learner Outcomes

After completing this Topic, you should be able to:

Explain the Socratic Method.
Summarize and critically evaluate Socratic ideas.
Recognize the importance of Socrates’ life and thought in the development of philosophy.
Compare and contrast the Jesuit notion of the role of the imagination in promoting philosophical reflection with the Socratic Method.

Learning Activities

Activity 1: Discussion?What is the Socratic Method?

Context

One of Socrates’ major contributions to Western philosophy is known as the Socratic method. As Chaffee (2013) explains, this “is a dynamic approach of questioning and intellectual analysis to draw answers out of people rather than lecture them” (p. 57). The method proceeds by asking a series of clarifying questions, which enable a person to identify the weaknesses in an argument. The advantage of this method is that it allows a person to arrive at his own conclusions. This activity will help familiarize you with the Socratic method and provide an opportunity to assess its strengths and weaknesses.

Task Description

After completing the required readings, craft a response addressing the following:

Briefly describe the Socratic method in your own words.
Identify what you believe to be the greatest strength and the greatest weakness of the Socratic method
What contemporary issue is most in need of the Socratic method today and why?
Apply the Socratic method to the contemporary issue you selected, and discuss how it helps to provide clarity and resolution to the problem.
Deliverable

Post your responses to the Discussion Forum titled for this activity. Your response should be substantive, reflecting your critical grasp of the reading material and incorporate your own viewpoint. Along with your initial posting, respond to one or more of your peers’ postings. Your response should encourage further discussion.

Activity 2: Discussion—The Socratic Method and Jesuit Values

Context

Regis University is a Jesuit institution. The Jesuits are a religious order of priests and brothers founded by St. Ignatius of Loyola in the sixteenth century. For more than 450 years, they have been involved in education. Regis is inspired by the values of Jesuit higher education. In this exercise, you will be asked to reflect on Fr. Adolfo Nicolás’ discussion on the need to develop the imagination in order to think deeply and to compare and contrast his ideas with the Socratic method.

Task Description

After reading the Nicolas, A. (2011) article and reviewing the Regis Jesuit values (see Readings and Resources section above), craft a post in which you:

Compare and contrast the Socratic method with Fr. Nicolás’ discussion of the Ignatian imagination. What similarities and differences can you identify?
How would you assess the value of the Ignatian imagination in the study of philosophy? Do you find it useful and why?
Go back to the contemporary issue you selected in Activity #1. Discuss how Fr. Nicolás’ approach could be used on it. Compare and contrast the Socratic method with Fr. Nicolás’ approach. How would you assess their strengths and weaknesses?

Activity 3: Discussion—Critical Reading of The Apology

Context

The Apology is one of the famous pieces of philosophical literature. The ideas espoused by Socrates are potent and profound, and people throughout history have gone to this text to consider its wisdom. This learning activity will allow you to read through this famous text, identify the key ideas, and assess their usefulness for today.

Task Description

After completing the required readings for this topic, craft a response to the following:

Identify a key Socratic idea from The Apology.
Explain the Socratic idea’s meaning.
Discuss the Socratic idea’s relevance in your life or in contemporary society.
Why do you think Socrates was put to death? Was it a just sentence?
